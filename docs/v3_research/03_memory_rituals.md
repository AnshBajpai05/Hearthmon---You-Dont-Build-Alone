# 03 — Memory Ritual Design

> **Central question: how does *placing something near the hearth* feel?** The ritual is the feature.
> Likely the **highest-ROI** research here — a week of this can beat a month of coding.

## The anti-pattern

```
Name:        [ ]
Category:    [ ]
Description: [ ]
```

A form. Flat, transactional, soulless. The data is identical to a sacred version — the *difference is
entirely in the ritual*.

## What we already have (from doctrine)

Lead with the **story, not the category** — *choose a door*:
- something it/they taught you
- a moment you'll never forget
- something it/they always meant to you
- a time it/they showed up for you

## The real questions

- What makes *recording why something matters* feel **sacred** rather than like filing? (Study
  **StoryCorps** — the act of recorded testimony, the "Great Questions" list, the single quiet booth.)
- **Pacing.** Should the first placement be slow, singular, undistracted — *one prompt, one breath*?
  Scarcity ([HEARTH_SHELF](../HEARTH_SHELF.md)) implies friction is a *feature* here.
- **Modality.** Typed words, or answering a *spoken* question? Voice carries weight text can't.
- **The moment of placing.** Is there a small ceremony — a sound, a settling animation, a warmth —
  that matters more than the data itself? (Physical analog: lighting a candle, setting down a photo.)

## Anti-goals (inherit from SOUL / EMOTIONAL_SAFETY)

No gamification. No streaks. No completion bars. No "you have 3 empty fields." The ritual must feel
good on the user's **worst day**, and it must never feel like a chore it would nag you to finish.

## Sources

StoryCorps; oral-history practice; *mono no aware* (the feeling of transience as you place
something); van Gennep, *rites of passage*; interaction design on "ceremony." See [07](07_sources_and_influences.md).
