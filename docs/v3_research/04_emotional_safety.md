# 04 — Emotional Safety (preservation vs. letting go)

> **Central question: how do you preserve without trapping?** This is *pure doctrine*, not
> implementation. It inherits everything in [EMOTIONAL_SAFETY.md](../EMOTIONAL_SAFETY.md) — V3 may
> never weaken those guardrails, only extend them.

## The five questions (from the design thread)

1. What should **never** be surfaced automatically?
2. What requires explicit **user intent** to surface?
3. What is **sacred** (handled differently from everything else)?
4. What is **reversible**?
5. What must be **exportable**?

## The intellectual anchor: continuing bonds

Modern grief research (**Klass, Silverman & Nickman, *Continuing Bonds*, 1996**) overturned the old
"grief work → detach → move on" model. Healthy grief is a **transformed, ongoing bond**, not an erasure.

→ This reframes **Release**: release is *not* deletion or forgetting. It is letting the bond **change
form**. The *Resting* state is continuing bonds made literal — the bond persists, quietly, transformed.

## The tension worth sitting in: the Bhagavad Gita

On its surface the Gita opposes a "hold close" sanctuary: it teaches **non-attachment** — *raga*
(clinging) causes suffering; in Ch. 2 grief for the dead is called misplaced, because the *atman*
(self) is eternal and only the *form* passes. But the Gita distinguishes **clinging to forms/outcomes**
from **love and devotion**. The reconciliation that could anchor this whole document:

> **Hold close without clinging.** Preserve the *meaning* (the lesson — what endures) without trapping
> the user in attachment to the *form* (the relationship/person/thing exactly as it was).

This is the same line as continuing bonds (*the bond transforms, it doesn't vanish*), arrived at from
the opposite direction. If both a grief scientist and a 2,000-year-old text point at the same
principle, it's probably load-bearing.

> ⚠️ **Humility.** This is a *thread to study*, not a verdict. Read good translations/commentaries
> (see [07](07_sources_and_influences.md)); do not flatten a sacred text into a UX maxim. Balance it
> with Stoic, Buddhist, and Japanese readings so it isn't one tradition oversimplified.

## Candidate doctrine (provisional)

- Heavy entries are **pull-first**: never auto-surfaced; the user comes to them.
- Nothing is ever **deleted**; everything is **reversible**; everything is **exportable**.
- The "sacred" tier (loss, the deceased) is *encountered*, never *summoned*, never *gamified*.
- **Associate, never confabulate.** Surfacing a true memory that *cues* another is the goal. The
  system must **never generate a blended/composite "memory"** that didn't happen. The brain mixes
  memories because it is *fallible*; a vault of real memories of real people exists precisely to
  **not** distort — fidelity is the whole value. (This is why "automatic memory blending" is rejected.)
